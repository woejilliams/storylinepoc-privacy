Station Zero: Privacy Policy

Effective November 10, 2026

Station Zero is a prototype app for trying new news-app ideas for local TV stations with TEGNA staff. You don’t need to sign in or create an account to use it, and it does not sell or share any personal information. The app contains no analytics, advertising or tracking code, and it does not access your location, contacts or microphone. It downloads what the station you pick (such as KARE 11 or KUSA 9) has published (its stories, and the content of its app concept, such as its team, shows, demo alerts and the stories in its home feed, including the articles you open) and their photos from Supabase, the service that hosts them. It does this when you open the app, when you return to it, when you pick a station, and when you tap Check for new Storylines. These downloads carry only what any web request does, such as your IP address and your device type and iOS version. They include no account, identifier or personal information, and Supabase may keep them in its logs for a short time for security and operations. What it downloads is saved on your device so it works offline. Some images, and the videos in the home feed's Shorts, load from the station's and TEGNA's own media servers (such as media.9news.com, media.tegna-media.com and assets.tegnaone.com), which receive the same basic request information. Other example images and video are built into the app. Signing in is optional and only for Studio users (TEGNA staff and people a Studio admin has added), to edit their station’s app. It uses your work email address and a one- time code sent to it, handled by Supabase, and keeps you signed in on the device until you sign out. Your edits are saved to your station’s draft in Studio, with your email address as the person who last changed it. If you’re signed in and choose to add a photo, the app uses the camera or your photo library only for the photo you take or pick, and only after iOS asks your permission. Before it’s uploaded, the photo is resized and its location data removed; it then goes, with the credit and rights you enter and your email address, into your station’s photo library on Supabase, where the image itself is public so the app can show it. When a show or person page has a live survey the station published, the app reads it, and if you vote it sends your answer, both to Supabase with a random number the app creates for your device the first time (kept on the device), so the survey can show your vote and each device votes once. The number isn’t linked to you, your Apple ID or any account. Your vote is counted, by answer and by where it came from (the app, the station’s website, its survey page or TV), together with votes from those places, and the station sees only the counts. If you share a survey, iOS passes a link to its page on vote.stationzero.dev to the app you choose. If you allow notifications, Station Zero shows the demo notifications you ask for; they are scheduled on your device, not sent from a server. Anything you type into the app concept's demo forms (a question, a story, a tip) stays on your device and is discarded when you close the form; nothing is sent. The station you pick, your settings, any bookmarks you save while signed in and any feedback you enter with the Report button are saved only on your device and are removed when you delete the app. If you tap a link, such as a source article on a station's website (for example kare11.com or 9news.com) or a photo credit on Wikimedia Commons, it opens in your browser. If you share a story, iOS passes it to the app you choose. Those sites and apps have their own privacy policies. Because the app is distributed through Apple's TestFlight, Apple may share your tester name and email, crash reports, basic usage information (such as installs and sessions) and any feedback you send through TestFlight with the developer, as described in Apple's TestFlight terms and privacy policy.
